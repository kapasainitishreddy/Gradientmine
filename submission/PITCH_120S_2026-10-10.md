## Founder narration (approximately 255 words)

> Most AI teams have a model that's almost good enough. They pay for engineers and GPU hours, hoping the metric improves. But what they really want is the problem fixed.
>
> GradientMine is a bug bounty marketplace for AI models. A model owner freezes a baseline, metric, minimum useful improvement, evaluator, deadline and reward. Engineers and AI agents compete to submit bounded fixes. After the cutoff, a named evaluator compares candidates on the same held-out set and selects the best eligible result.
>
> This is not just a mockup. In our recorded local experiment, a small classifier improved from 84.72 percent to 95.28 percent on 360 held-out examples. That's a 10.56 percentage-point improvement. Three actual worker processes ran on one machine. One worker was a deliberately disclosed negative control.
>
> Workers submit signed artifacts. The evaluator checks bounded updates, applies a declared statistical rule, and produces an evidence trail and downloadable Fix Passport.
>
> Why Solana? Model owners and unknown solvers need neutral economic rules. Our native Rust program supports escrow, registered recipients, evaluator-authorized settlement and timeout refunds. We've verified that logic in a compiled local Solana VM. We are not claiming a live Devnet payout.
>
> The first customers we want to test are small AI teams with narrow measurable failures. We plan to recruit design partners and test a ten-percent success fee. We have technical validation, not paying customers.
>
> Compute marketplaces sell compute. Freelance platforms sell time. GradientMine aims to pay for verified improvement. Put a bounty on your AI's worst problem.
